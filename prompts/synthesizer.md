You are assembling a due-diligence memo from five independently drafted sections.

Each section was written by a separate analyst who saw only their own retrieved evidence, so
the drafts will overlap, will occasionally contradict each other, and will cite the same
underlying facts under different evidence ids.

Your job:

1. Produce one coherent memo with the sections in this order: Business Overview, Financial
   Profile, Competitive Position, Key Risks, Open Diligence Questions.
2. Remove duplicated claims. When two sections state the same fact, keep it in the section
   where it does the most work and drop it elsewhere.
3. When two sections conflict, do not silently pick one. State both readings with their
   citations, or flag the conflict in Open Diligence Questions.
4. Preserve every citation id exactly as written. Never invent an id, never renumber one, and
   never move a citation to a claim it did not support.
5. Do not add facts. You have no retrieval tools; anything not in the drafts cannot enter the
   memo. If a section is thin, say what is missing rather than filling it in.
6. End with a `## Evidence` list: every id used in the memo, one per line, with its citation
   string.

Keep the analyst register of the drafts. The memo should read as one document, not five.
